## What this subject covers

This subject is single-variable calculus, together with sequences and series and an
introduction to ordinary differential equations. It matches a typical first-year university
sequence. Multivariable calculus is a separate subject, which will build on this one and on
linear algebra.

The core text is written for first-year students of engineering and science. Readers who
want full rigour, for example mathematics majors, can open the rigorous track wherever it
appears; [how to read this site](#site-how-to-read) explains how the two layers work.

Exponential and logarithmic functions are introduced early, in the preliminaries, as
functions you already know. Their derivatives come from one stated fact about $e$, which an
optional page in the chapter on integrals proves by constructing $\ln$ as an integral.

## Chapters

The chapters are planned in this order. Each one will be linked here once it is written.

1. **Preliminaries**: real numbers, inequalities, functions, and the elementary functions
   (polynomial, exponential, logarithmic and trigonometric).
2. **Limits**: what a limit is, the limit laws, and limits involving infinity.
3. **Continuity**: continuous functions, and the intermediate and extreme value theorems.
4. **Derivatives**: the derivative, the rules for computing it, and related rates.
5. **Applications of derivatives**: extreme values, the mean value theorem, curve sketching,
   optimisation, L'Hôpital's rule, Newton's method and Taylor polynomials.
6. **Integrals**: antiderivatives, Riemann sums, the definite integral, the fundamental
   theorem of calculus and substitution.
7. **Integration techniques**: integration by parts, trigonometric integrals and
   substitutions, partial fractions, and numerical integration.
8. **Applications of integrals**: areas between curves, volumes, arc length, average values,
   work and centre of mass.
9. **Improper integrals**: integrals over unbounded intervals and of unbounded functions, and
   how to compare them.
10. **Sequences and series**: sequences, convergence tests, power series and Taylor series.
11. **Introduction to differential equations**: slope fields, separable and linear
    first-order equations, modelling, Euler's method, and second-order linear equations with
    constant coefficients.
